# Video script and shot list

Target: **3 minutes or less**, about 420 spoken words. Every figure below is from
[NUMBERS.md](NUMBERS.md). Do not improvise a number on camera.

## Before you record

- [ ] Start a **fresh run** 10–20 minutes before recording. The voice link is signed on each
      read so it will not expire, but a recent run gives a live Step Functions execution for
      the console shots.
- [ ] Check the dashboard and the API are up: `curl -s $API_URL/health`
- [ ] 1080p, browser zoom 110%, cursor visible.
- [ ] Close all notifications (Slack, mail, Telegram desktop) and hide bookmarks.
- [ ] Mic test: record 10 seconds and listen back.
- [ ] Phone mirrored to the screen, or filmed, for the Telegram part.
- [ ] **Do not show:** the AWS account menu, the Telegram bot token, the demo key, any full ARN.
- [ ] Record the live section twice and keep the better take.
- [ ] Keep the AWS console segment **at least 20 seconds**.

## Script

| Time | Screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:20** | Dashboard **Sites** page (or a worksite photo) | "Outdoor workers in India work through heat that air-temperature alerts do not describe well. Heat stress depends on humidity, sun and wind together, not just temperature. We built ShramShield. It turns open weather data into a work-and-rest plan for each site, tells the supervisor in Hindi, and makes sure somebody acknowledges it." |
| **0:20–0:50** | **Site page** for Chennai: scroll the hourly strip, click the peak hour | "This is a Chennai site. Every hour has a WBGT heat-stress score, computed with the Liljegren model from ECMWF's thermofeel library, using humidity, wind and solar radiation. The level comes from published ACGIH screening limits — not from anything we invented. At the peak hour here the level is **[read the level off the screen]**, which means at most **[read the minutes]** minutes of work in that hour, and the rest in shade." |
| **0:50–1:20** | **Backtest** page | "We replayed April to June 2024 for four Indian cities. Four thousand and four work hours. Two thousand and ninety-seven of them were RED or STOP in our model. An air-temperature alert at 40 degrees would have missed one thousand eight hundred and sixty-four of those — **88.9 percent**. Look at the contrast: Delhi had 268 hours above 40 degrees but 320 dangerous hours. Chennai had six hours above 40 degrees and 679 dangerous hours. Dry heat trips a thermometer. Humid heat does not. This compares two methods on the same data — it is not a medical validation." |
| **1:20–2:10** | Click **Send today's plan now** → cut to the **phone** → back to the **run page** | "Now the live part. I send today's plan. On the supervisor's phone a Hindi voice message arrives, generated by Amazon Polly, with the plan and an Acknowledge button. Behind it, Step Functions is holding a task token and waiting — not polling, not burning compute. If nobody answers inside the time limit it escalates to the backup contact. I'll press Acknowledge… and the run closes. Status: acknowledged, who acknowledged it, and by which route. The whole timeline is recorded." |
| **2:10–2:40** | AWS console: Step Functions graph → DynamoDB item → Lambda list → CloudFormation resources | "On AWS: API Gateway and Lambda serve the API. DynamoDB stores sites, plans, runs and the task tokens. Step Functions runs the wait-and-escalate workflow. Polly speaks Hindi, S3 holds the audio behind presigned links, EventBridge can schedule the morning run, and the whole stack is defined in one SAM template." |
| **2:40–3:00** | **Method** page, then the GitHub repo | "The safety numbers are deterministic physics and published limits — not an AI guess. And we say what it is not: not a measurement, not medical advice, not clinically validated. The Hindi is machine-drafted and still waiting on a native speaker. Open source, MIT, built during this hackathon. Thank you." |

## Words to avoid on camera

"Prevents", "saves lives", "clinically validated", "certified", "accurate to X percent",
"AI-powered" (the AI wrote code; it does not decide the safety numbers), and anything implying
the Hindi was reviewed.
